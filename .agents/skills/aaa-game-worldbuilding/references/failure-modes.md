# Failure Modes

- Calling a random generated result a designed level without route, landmark,
  gameplay, or composition work.
- Replacing a supported advanced natural-world system with primitive forests,
  a flat water slab, or generic fog.
- Regenerating a complete world after every minor time, bookmark, or post edit.
- Using dense fog, black shadows, or crushed exposure to hide absent detail.
- Leaving bare near-field terrain, uniform scatter grids, repeated hero trees,
  smooth hero cliffs, or texture-only streambeds.
- Raising every setting to ultra without GPU intent or performance inspection.
- Removing water, vegetation, atmosphere, or landmarks as the first
  optimization response instead of targeting the responsible cost.
- Claiming a generation, screenshot result, GPU timing, feature, or UE5 parity
  that has not been confirmed by a real tool result.
- Creating a procedural open world for a compact interior or unrelated
  micro-edit.
