# AAA Environment Quality Pillars

## Geometry, not flat illusion

Near-camera terrain needs geological breaks, streambeds, root flare, rocks,
debris, and surface transitions that read in silhouette. Do not treat a color
map, smooth hero cliff, flat water plane, or a repeated primitive tree as a
finished ecosystem.

## Light transport

Showcase shadows must remain readable. Preserve skylight, bounce color,
foliage translucency, environmental tint, local contrast, and subject
separation. Never hide unfinished detail in black shadows, crushed exposure,
or opaque fog.

## Nothing is bare

Every major surface class needs an intentional occupancy or weathering story:
cliffs, boulder crevices, meadows, tree bases, water margins, roads, ruins,
snow lines, and shorelines. Bare areas are valid only when they support the
composition or gameplay.

## Distance holds

The horizon needs layered terrain, forest structure, atmospheric depth, and
landmarks. Do not use fog as a blanket to conceal absent distant content.

## Art direction

Generation serves composition, color script, visual hierarchy, mood,
landmarks, and traversal. A technically complex but random-looking scene is
not finished.

## The world moves

Use wind, water, clouds, snow, pollen, leaves, fog, and ambient movers where
the active tools support them. Motion should reinforce scale and mood without
turning the frame into noise.
